03MACHINE LEARNING · CUSTOMER RETENTION

Customer Churn
Prediction Analysis

Analyzed 10,000 retail-bank customer records and built classification models to identify customers at risk of leaving, translating model performance into a retention-focused decision framework.

10KCustomer records
~20%Customer churn rate
86.0%Random Forest accuracy

THE BUSINESS QUESTION

Which customer characteristics are associated with churn, and can a predictive model help a bank identify at-risk customers early enough to support targeted retention efforts?

APPROACH

Explored customer demographics and account behavior, engineered financial-ratio features, prepared numerical model inputs, trained Logistic Regression and Random Forest classifiers, and evaluated performance beyond accuracy.

MODELING WORKFLOW

Explore 10K Records→Engineer Features→Train Models→Evaluate Metrics→Retention Insights

MODEL PERFORMANCE

Random Forest delivered stronger overall classification performance.

Accuracy alone can hide missed churners, so the comparison includes precision, recall, and F1 score.

THRESHOLD ANALYSIS

Optimizing for the cost of missed churners.

The baseline Logistic Regression had low recall. Lowering its decision threshold to 0.34 increased recall to approximately 42.1%, illustrating the trade-off between identifying more churners and generating more false positives.

KEY SIGNALS

What was associated with churn?

The notebook identifies age, number of products, account balance, activity status, and engineered financial ratios among important predictive signals. These are model associations, not proof of causal effects.

BUSINESS APPLICATION

Move from reactive churn reporting to proactive retention targeting.

Predicted churn risk can help prioritize customers for retention outreach. The analysis also identifies future work including cross-validation, gradient boosting, deployment, and ongoing model monitoring.

04MACHINE LEARNING · HEALTH ANALYTICS

Heart Disease
Prediction Analysis

Built and compared classification models for heart-disease prediction, combining exploratory health analysis, categorical encoding, robust scaling, imbalance handling with SMOTE, threshold evaluation, and model comparison.

93.01%Random Forest accuracy

91.92%Recall
92.94%F1 score

THE ANALYTICAL QUESTION

Can demographic, lifestyle, and health indicators be used to classify heart-disease status, and which tested model provides the strongest balance of predictive metrics on this dataset?

APPROACH

Explored distributions and relationships across health variables, encoded categorical features, used RobustScaler and SMOTE to address scale and class imbalance, then compared Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest classifiers.

ML WORKFLOW

Explore Health Data→Encode & Scale→Balance with SMOTE→Train 3 Models→Evaluate & Compare

MODEL COMPARISON

EXPLORATORY FINDINGS

Health risk is multi-factorial.

The analysis explored age, BMI, physical and mental health, sleep, stroke, kidney disease, diabetes, smoking, and other indicators. Within the dataset, older age groups showed higher heart-disease incidence, and stroke, kidney disease, and diabetes showed notable co-occurrence patterns.

IMBALANCE STRATEGY

Designed around the minority class.

Because heart-disease cases were imbalanced, the project tested SMOTE-based resampling strategies and emphasized precision, recall, F1, ROC analysis, and confusion matrices rather than relying on accuracy alone.

APPLICATION & LIMITATIONS

A predictive analytics study — not a clinical diagnostic system.

The project demonstrates how classification can support risk stratification research and model comparison. Reported results are specific to the project's preprocessing and evaluation setup and should not be interpreted as validated clinical performance.
